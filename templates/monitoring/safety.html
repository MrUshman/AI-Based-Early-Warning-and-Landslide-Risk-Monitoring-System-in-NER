1. Before Travelling in the Hills

  • Check this portal: Confirm current risk status of your highway corridor (NH-10, NH-6, NH-29, etc.).
  • Avoid night travel: Never drive on hilly highways at night during heavy monsoon downpours when slopes cannot be seen.
  • Pack essentials: Keep extra drinking water, dry food, torch, and power bank in your vehicle.
  • Listen to local advisories: Respect police barricades and BRO traffic restrictions without arguing.

2. Slope Failure Warning Signs

  • Road or ground cracks: New tension cracks appearing on the asphalt surface or hillside cut.
  • Falling pebbles or stones: Sudden trickling of small stones is often the earliest signal of a major slide.
  • Sudden muddy water: Clear mountain streams or roadside drains turning suddenly muddy or brown.
  • Tilting trees & poles: Trees, utility poles, or retaining walls leaning downhill.

3. If a Landslide Occurs

  • Do NOT cross debris: Never attempt to drive or walk across freshly deposited mud; secondary slides follow quickly.
  • Move away from slope: Move quickly away from the path of debris to an open, stable ridge.
  • Warn other motorists: Switch on vehicle hazard lights and signal oncoming vehicles to halt at safe distance.
  • Call 112 immediately: Report your highway milestone and whether any vehicles are trapped.
Special Monsoon Advisory for the North Eastern Region

The North Eastern Region experiences intense monsoon spells from June to September. Heavy rainfall exceeding 100mm in 24 hours creates immense pore-water pressure inside Himalayan weathered schists and shales.

For real-time updates during extreme weather events, tune into local All India Radio broadcasts, follow state DDMA notifications on Twitter/X, and refer to this early warning portal for updated slope risk scores.
